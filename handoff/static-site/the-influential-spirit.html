Day 1

Following to Lead

“Then Jesus said to them, ‘Follow Me, and I will make you become fishers of men.’”

Mark 1:17

Far from polished pews and familiar temple hymnbooks, the lakeshore presented a raw, unfiltered scene. The sun beat down on drying fish, filling the air with the pungent aroma of wet rope, human sweat, and the salty mist of the Sea of Galilee. This was everyday labor, unadorned and demanding.

For Simon and Andrew, it was the everyday routine. They were busy, tired, and dealing with the same ordinary pressures you know. These fishermen had bills to pay, families to feed, and a business to keep afloat. Their worries were as real as yours.

Then, right in the middle of their ordinary day, Jesus stepped onto their dock and disrupted their setup.

To understand why Simon and Andrew dropped their nets without hesitation, we have to look at what had already taken place between them and the Master.

During their first meeting, Jesus gave Simon a new prophetic name, Cephas, meaning ‘rock’ or ‘stone.’ So, He called men who already knew His voice. Because they had met Him before. The Lord then invited them to take that relationship to a committed, no-return level.

The choice they faced stares at you and me today. Were they to cling to their safety nets, or risk everything to follow Jesus?

Their first encounter with Jesus laid a foundation of trust. But from that point, He called them to step up, to grow, and to commit themselves. For three years, Jesus invested in them as if the entire mission on earth depended on them, and to a great extent, it did. He taught, corrected, and shaped them into the leaders God intended them to become.

The Rabbi’s Choice

In declaring, ‘I will make you fishers of men,’ Jesus invited His disciples into a daily journey of conversion rather than a sudden title.

Jesus handed them a new dream, bigger than anything they had ever imagined. The mission was bold. He invited these common fishermen to join Him, not only for their benefit, but to fulfill a purpose only He could help them achieve, with their permission, of course. They did not know where the journey led, yet He showed them who they were to become if they dared to trust Him.

During that period, ‘Follow Me’ was a rabbi’s call to a student. Jesus reversed the usual dynamic in which students pursued esteemed teachers. He initiated the call to these ordinary people who were tired and worn from their work.

To transition from fishing to evangelizing, they needed to stay with the Teacher. Nothing was casual about this arrangement, so they couldn’t take anything for granted. Their influence wasn’t a result of their own efforts alone. Following Christ gave their effective leadership a solid foundation.

Carrying the Rabbi’s Scent

Virtues are not just taught; they are caught. People learn by observing, not merely by listening. The disciples discovered that their ability to bring others to Christ came from knowing Christ at close range. They did not center the season on memorizing a set of methods. It focused more on capturing the heart of the Master and learning in a way that changes the learner, whereby the change is the proof of learning.

Returning home after a full day in a charcoal pit requires no explanation for your whereabouts. The smoke clings to your clothes. The smell lingers on your skin. You bring the scent of charcoal home with you.

This also applies to spiritual authority. And in the next chapter, we’ll find Peter and John, lacking formal education, astounding the council.

How often do we crave Jesus’ power yet neglect to sit at His feet and learn from Him? We hope our words will change lives, but we have not let Him change us first. Jesus is saying, stop striving for influence in your own strength. Walk with Me first, and true impact will result.

Jesus asked His first disciples to spend their days in His company. He asks the same of you.

Walking It Out

Daily Reflection

What traps keep you from following Jesus? Is it fear, career ambition, or a craving for control?

The Influence Challenge

Set aside ten minutes today. Turn off your phone and silence mental distractions. In stillness, pray: “Heavenly Father, I give You my agenda. Position me where You desire.” Discipleship is not about checking a religious box. It means finding calm in His presence and following the Spirit’s guidance. It’s wanting to become the person He wants you to be.

Prayer

Loving Father, shape me, mold me, and use me as a vessel for Your glory. In the matchless name of Jesus, Amen.

Daily Declaration · Following the Shepherd

Before anything else, I am a follower of Christ. His power flows through my life as I submit to His authority. God is shaping me into a fisher of souls and a life-changer.

The tension

Modern culture mistakes visibility for influence.

Titles. Followers. Recognition. A promotion. Applause you can screenshot.

A platform can put someone in front of a crowd without doing a single thing to their character. God is not just handing out platforms. He is shaping the people who will stand on them, because unformed hands break what they hold.

That is the tension this book lives inside: not whether you should be visible, but who you are before, and while, you become that way.

The person behind the influence

Christ forms the person.

The Spirit does the empowering. Everything you actually want, credibility, weight, a voice people trust, grows out of that, never the other way around.

These pages will not teach you how to get noticed. That is the subject of another book. They walk with you toward becoming someone worth following.

Your 30-day path

Three pillars of kingdom influence.

Christlike influence is forged when divine authority meets steady character in the ordinary spaces of your workday.

I

Days 1–10

Kingdom Authority

Move out of ordinary drift. Learn to bring Christ into the small daily decisions everyone else is calling neutral. The meeting, the reply, the money, the margin, the door you almost walked past.

II

Days 11–20

Formed Character

The quiet integrity that has to be there before the crowd arrives. What survives when no one is watching, and what breaks under a pressure your gift can no longer hide.

III

Days 21–30

Marketplace Impact

Your desk, your clinic, your business, your kitchen table. The ordinary places where obedience actually meets Monday, and where your competence becomes a form of love for the people you serve.

Inside the thirty days

The 30 days.

Thirty days. One question underneath every one of them: who are you becoming while you become visible? Carried through Scripture, character, prayer, and the daily work of a life.

The days are not the same day repeated. They build. Each day is short enough for a morning: a Scripture, an honest reading of it, and a question that follows you into the day.

Days 1–10 i

The ground.

The days go down to foundations: what God is actually doing when he slows a life that was moving too fast, or closes a door you were certain he had opened.

Days 11–20 ii

The weight.

The quiet authority that grows in a person who has learned to interpret God's hand before rushing to respond to it.

Days 21–30 iii

The hand-off.

Day 30 is not the finish line. It's a hand-off. You take the work into your office, your business, and the places where your week is actually spent.

Inside the reading

What the days practice.

Nothing here is a formula. It is a way of seeing what God is doing, then responding to it.

  • Character who you are when no one is watching.
  • Prayer whether your public life stays tethered to a private one.
  • The Holy Spirit what dependence on God looks like outside the dramatic moments.
  • Competence how excellence and reliance on God work together, not against each other.
  • Leadership whether you can lead before anyone hands you a title.
  • Pressure what difficulty reveals about who you have actually become.
  • Service what happens when influence turns into lifting someone else.
  • Legacy what is left standing after the applause stops.

Who this is for

You are already influencing someone.

The question is who you are while you do it. Read the list slowly. One of these is you.

  • §You lead something, even if it is small: a class, a cell, a WhatsApp group, a pulpit on rotation.
  • §You were given influence before you felt ready for it, and you would rather be formed than flattered.
  • §You are tired of chasing visibility and calling the exhaustion faithfulness.
  • §You want to hear God for yourself, not just carry another person's fire.
  • §You suspect God cares more about who you are becoming than about how many people are watching.
  • §You will do slow work if it lasts. Thirty days slow.

Questions readers are already asking

Questions readers are already asking.

Do I need to be a leader to read it?

No. Influence here has nothing to do with a title. It's about ordinary responsibility, character, and the people who are already watching your life.

Is this a leadership book?

It's more than that. The Influential Spirit is a devotional about spiritual formation. Leadership, work, and influence are where that formation gets tested.

Is the book against platforms?

No. Ambition, visibility, leadership, none of that is the target here. The question underneath the whole book is simpler and harder: who are you becoming while you become visible?

Is there a journal?

Yes, in the Complete Formation Edition.

Can I use it with a group?

Yes. The Formation Bundle and Complete Formation Edition both include the six-session Group Study Guide.

Is the audiobook included?

Yes. The Formation Bundle includes the author-narrated audiobook.

Will this work if I'm not in ministry?

Yes, especially. Most of the book is written to the person whose "pulpit" is a desk, a classroom, a clinic, a business, a boardroom, a kitchen table. Ministry is one field. This book is for every field where a Christian is asked to represent Christ well.

How long is each day?

Short enough for a morning. A scripture, an honest reading of it, a reflection question, a practice for the day, a prayer, and a declaration. Most readers finish a day in ten to fifteen minutes, then carry the question into whatever their Monday holds.

How is this different from your first book, Becoming an Influence?

Becoming an Influence focused on God's call for us to lead. This edition, renamed The Influential Spirit, answers a different question: what manner of person must we become to represent Christ in modern society? Formation first, then the platform. The Spirit's work in you is what propels the work through you. It is an expanded response for a moment when we are all mistaking visibility for spiritual reality.
