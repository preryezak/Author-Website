## 2. Sentence and structure rules

1. **No em-dashes**, and no hyphens used as dashes.
2. **No binary contrasts**, including the subtle ones: "It's not X, it's Y", "He doesn't say X. He says Y", "Not because X, but because Y", "Don't read it as X. Read it as Y". State the point directly. A contrast inside an exact NKJV quotation stays as written.
3. **No fragments.** "Two days. That's all." becomes a full sentence.
4. **Point first.** Each section opens with its claim; the story or explanation follows.
5. **A shareable line every 60 to 90 seconds**, marked `[QUOTE CARD]`.
6. **No dragging.** Every paragraph earns its place and makes its point quickly.
7. **Vary list lengths.** Avoid always grouping in threes.
8. **Natural spoken English only.** No construction you wouldn't say aloud to a congregation.
9. **No empty upbeat closings** ("the future looks bright") and no unnecessary summaries.
10. **Hedge only real scholarly disagreements**, and name them plainly.
11. **Simple reading level.** Plan on about 130 spoken words a minute: roughly 3,500 words per podcast and 850 to 900 per daily.

## 3. Theology and integrity rules

1. **Gospel edge in every piece:** Christ, the cross and the Holy Spirit are named explicitly. Change comes from the Spirit, never from self-talk or willpower.
2. **Context before application.** Say what a passage meant first (e.g. 1 Timothy 3:7 is about overseers), then apply the principle.
3. **Flag interpretive disagreements** (e.g. Daniel's reasons, "day of visitation", agapao/phileo).
4. **Greek and Hebrew claims** are marked "check against your tools before recording".
5. **Quote the NKJV exactly.** If a verse contains an em-dash, quote up to the dash or choose another verse.
6. **Never name or hint at a real fallen leader.** Stay at pattern level. Companies can be named from public records (Wells Fargo, Theranos); individuals are not.
7. **Flag prosperity-gospel framing** wherever it appears, and challenge it.
8. **Safety lines:** when abuse, shame or despair comes up, point people to counselling, safety first, reporting abuse, and emergency services. Forgiveness never means going back into danger.
9. **Scripture does not approve of slavery.** When a "bondservant" text is used, say so (Col 4:1).

## 4. Stories and sources

1. **No invented scenarios or composites.** Use Scripture narrative, verified public stories (East African first), pastoral windows (real, anonymised, with permission) or your own moments, within the agreed ration of three.
2. **Every statistic and story is sourced** from a page actually opened, and the sources are listed in the audit notes.
3. **Paraphrase unless you have the exact wording.** Quote only what was verified.

- The podcast releases first; the dailies go deeper into one part of it each day. Thursday and Friday may refer to "this week's podcast".

- **Outline first, then content**, for every week.
- **Repeated segments are worded fresh each week** to fit the theme: the comment question, the book line, the giving line, and the comment callback.
- **Audio-first:** anything shown on screen is also said.
